# Roots research audit — 9 October 2026

All 325 vocabulary cards were reviewed individually and across the complete deck. All source words, meanings, categories, and conjugated forms remain represented.

Current selection: 975 Memory Anchor candidates (three per card; the learner picks one), 262 Roots aids, and 151 explicitly identified components. Origins are not filled merely to reach a quota.

## Research method and limits

Consulted the National Institute of Korean Language’s Korean Basic Dictionary and language-advice pages, then Wiktionary word entries, morphology entries, and individual Hanja entries. NIKL became intermittently inaccessible during research; Wiktionary supplied most remaining word-level evidence. The source registry links the exact pages used. Sources remain accessible in each Roots reveal, including evidence for the displayed related words. No external service is needed to study.

Classifications describe the supported components, not an exhaustive history of transmission. Sino-Korean words may have entered through Japanese; no route is asserted unless useful and supported. Modern word formation and conjugation are kept distinct from historical etymology. Phonetic mnemonics are invented learning aids, explicitly labeled as such.

For multiword cuisines, genres, and sentences, reviewed their constituent dictionary entries and particles rather than claiming the entire expression is a dictionary headword. In genre expressions without an accessible genre entry, only the verified 영화 component is given an origin. Source meanings provide the plain-language phrase breakdown.

**Unconfirmed origin entries:** 엔지니어 and 일렉트로닉 retain familiar English sound cues but make no donor-language claim. Their failed lookup URLs and omission reasons are recorded. The source-language path for 발라드 was not resolved; the Roots section is omitted. This audit does not claim every historical origin is settled.

**Historical restraint:** no speculative older analysis is displayed for 수박, 김치, 복숭아, 언니, 돈, or the deeper origins of 과일 and 재미. Some references propose older derivations, but the selected aids use modern, defensible components or omit the origin. 가방 is linked to Japanese kaban; differing accounts of its earlier source are not repeated.
